Tool-use is the drivetrain, not the engine

A bigger model is a bigger engine. It does nothing for you until something transfers that power to the ground. In practice the transfer layer is tool-use, and almost every agent I have shipped improved more from fixing that layer than from swapping the model underneath.

DIAGRAM
engine → gearbox

Torque you cannot deliver is not power. Same with reasoning you cannot act on.

Three fixes, in order

  1. Return the smallest useful payload.
  2. Make failures legible — a typed error beats a stack trace.
  3. Log every call and read the logs weekly.1

# return one field, not the whole record

    row = db.get(order_id)
    return Status(state=row.state, eta=row.eta)

Hi — I’m Seidel, and this is my workshop.

I build agentic applications: software that plans, calls tools, and gets something done without me watching. Most of what I learn doing that ends up here as a post, and most of what I finish ends up deployed on a subdomain.

The shop framing is a joke I decided to take seriously. A robot outfitter on a distant planet, selling retrofits to passing ships, is a fair description of what this work feels like: someone arrives with a vehicle, the vehicle needs a part it does not have yet, and the interesting problem is the fitting.

Everything is written in markdown, versioned in git, and published with no tracking and no newsletter popup. If something here is wrong, mail me and I will fix it in public.

Right now

  • Making Forge good enough to open up.
  • Reading about long-horizon planning and failing to be convinced.
  • Open to consulting on agent evaluation.
